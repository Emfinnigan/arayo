01. What Users Actually See

Five surfaces, one trip.

Most discovery apps are one feature: a directory, a chat, a feed. Jinjja stitches together five surfaces that cover the entire arc of a foreign visitor's trip — discovering, planning, eating, sharing, and connecting with locals.

Each surface is described below alongside a live mockup of how it renders in the app.

Feature 01

The AI friend

A user types "where can I drink in Hongdae tonight" or "what's the best food in Jamsil" — and Jinjja responds the way a Seoul-local friend would over text. It also handles the Korean versions of the same prompts: "홍대에서 술 마실 수 있는 곳" or "잠실 맛집 추천".

Under the hood, Claude Haiku reasons about the prompt, calls one of four tools, queries our curated database (with a fallback to Korean-language sources), and streams back a conversational response with structured place cards below.

  • Multi-turn context — follow-ups work ("actually somewhere cheaper")
  • Bilingual input — accepts English, Korean, or mixed
  • Anonymous use — the full app works pre-signup

Feature 03

The hosts directory — no one eats alone

The biggest unmet need for foreigners visiting Korea isn't finding a restaurant. It's that so many great spots are group-only — Korean BBQ has minimum orders, soju is for sharing, the late-night experience requires a crew.

Hosts solves this. Foreigners and Koreans opt into a directory by neighborhood and interest. The AI matches them — a solo traveler wanting Korean BBQ tonight gets matched with a Korean who hosts BBQ nights, or another foreigner looking for the same.

  • Two-sided directory — Korean locals (foodies, students, retirees) + visiting foreigners
  • AI-mediated matching — Haiku reads both profiles and the venue to suggest pairings
  • Built on the ground — Korean host density that foreign-built apps can't replicate

Feature 04

Routes — a full night in one tap

For "plan my Saturday in Hongdae" prompts, the AI wires 2–5 venues into a sequenced itinerary with walking time between stops. Maps to the Korean dining tradition of 1차 (meal) → 2차 (drinks) → 3차 (after-spot).

Users can re-open routes, share them with friends, swap individual stops without losing the rest.

  • Cuisine pairing logic — what works as a 2차 after a 1차
  • Walking-time sequencing — each stop within reasonable distance
  • Editable — swap any single stop without losing the route

Feature 05

Collections — your Korea database

Users save places they love into named collections — "Date night spots", "Hangover cures near the office", "Things to try with my mom". Every user is essentially building their own personal Korean database of bars, restaurants, cafes, and clubs they want to come back to.

Users can also save Instagram posts and TikToks of places they spotted — paste the link, our AI discovery tool identifies the restaurant, and it lands directly in their Collection. The "I saw this place on TikTok but can't find it" problem is solved.

People can save before signing up — and when they do sign up, all their saved places carry over. No signup wall blocking first-use. The friction is gone.

  • A personal database — curated by the user, for the user
  • Save from Instagram & TikTok — AI finds the venue from the link
  • Saves persist pre-signup — frictionless first session
  • Shareable — send a Collection to a friend coming to visit
  • Feedback loop — what people save informs what we curate next

02. How the AI Actually Works

Haiku doesn't just talk — it acts.

The brain of Jinjja is Claude Haiku running on a Next.js Edge Function on Vercel. The mobile app sends prompts in, the function builds a system prompt with the user's location and history, and Haiku is given four tools it can call.

The intelligence isn't in generating words — it's in which tool Haiku picks based on the prompt. That's the reasoning step.

01
search_places
Curated DB lookup
Default first call. Queries our hand-vetted Postgres database with filters for location, cuisine, hours, and category.
02
discover_places
Korea-native fallback
Triggered if the curated DB doesn't have enough. Hits Kakao Local API live, mines Naver blog snippets, synthesizes a fresh editorial.
03
build_route
Multi-stop itinerary
Wires 2–5 venues into a sequenced timeline with walking distance and cuisine pairing logic. The 1차→2차→3차 builder.
04
ask_clarification
Ask the user back
For ambiguous prompts, Haiku asks one short question rather than guessing. Mimics how a friend would actually text.
03. What Makes Jinjja Different

The AI alone is a thin wrapper. The system is the product.

Anyone can call an LLM API. What makes Jinjja different is the combination of assets and features that no foreign-built competitor can replicate — a curated dataset, a two-sided hosts network, Korea-specific AI intelligence, a personal AI that learns each user, consolidated content discovery, and the ability to save from anywhere.

No. 1
A curated, growing database
100+ hand-vetted venues with curated dishes, editorials, video reels, and rich metadata. The AI handles discovery, but the long-term content asset is the curated set itself.
Every time the AI has to fall back to Kakao for a missing venue, that gap gets logged — and feeds the next round of curation automatically.
No. 2
A two-sided hosts network
Korean locals and visiting foreigners opt in to host or be matched. This solves a real problem — group-only Korean food experiences (BBQ, soju, late-night) that solo travelers can't access alone.
Building host density on the ground in Korea, neighborhood by neighborhood. Not something a foreign team can ship overnight.
No. 3
Korea-specific AI intelligence
Deep dish knowledge and how to order it — so users can point at menus and know what to ask for. The Seoul-friend voice. Korean keyword auto-translation. The nightlife hours rules (Korean clubs open after 22:00).
All of this is iterated taste from years in Korea. It cannot be copied from a generic translation API.
No. 4
A compounding personal AI
Every user gets their own agent that remembers and learns their tastes — just like a Korean friend would. The more they use it, the more it knows: what they liked, what they didn't, what they want to try next.
The longer Jinjja runs, the better every user's experience gets — automatically, without manual rework. That's a compounding asset, not a one-time recommendation.
No. 5
Content discovery, consolidated
Users no longer need to scroll through Instagram and TikTok to find places. Jinjja consolidates discovery into one feed — curated reels, user saves, AI recommendations — all surfaced in one place, instantly browsable.
Discovery time goes from hours of cross-app scrolling to seconds. That's the entire reason people use the app daily.
No. 6
Save from anywhere → find it for you
Users can paste in an Instagram or TikTok link of a place they saw, and our AI discovery tool identifies the restaurant, pulls the details, and adds it directly to their Collection.
No more "I saw this place on TikTok but I can't remember where it was." Jinjja becomes the home for every place a user has ever wanted to try in Korea.
